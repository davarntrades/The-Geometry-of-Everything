### Galileo — 1610

```
ASSUMPTION EVERYONE HELD:
  Heavy objects fall faster than light ones.
  Aristotle said so. Everyone agreed.
  It was obvious. You could feel it intuitively.
  Nobody had tested it.

WHAT HE CROSSED:
  The boundary between common sense and measurement.
  Between intuition and geometry.

THE ANSWER:
  Drop them both. Same speed.
  The geometry doesn't care about your intuition.
  Mass doesn't determine fall rate.
  Gravity is uniform.
  The principle was always larger than Aristotle's domain.

WHAT WAS REQUIRED:
  Nobody tested it.
  Galileo went to the tower and dropped the balls.
  The boundary between "everyone knows" and "the measurement says"
  is where every scientific revolution begins.

DIAGRAM:

  THE INTUITIVE PREDICTION (Aristotle):

  Heavy ball ●  →  falls FAST  ──────────────► ground
  Light ball ○  →  falls SLOW  ─────────────────────────► ground

  THE GEOMETRIC REALITY (Galileo):

  Heavy ball ●  →  falls      ──────────────► ground
  Light ball ○  →  falls      ──────────────► ground
                   (same speed, same time, same geometry)

  The geometry has no preference for mass in free fall.
  Same rate for everything.
  Intuition was wrong.
  Measurement was right.
  The measurement always was right.

WHAT IT PRODUCED:
  The scientific method.
  The principle that measurement beats intuition.
  The foundation of physics.
  Without Galileo — no Newton.
```
